4Valuation Methodology

The internal appraisal was conducted in accordance with Section 6A of the Valuation of Land Act 1916, which requires land to be valued as if it were vacant (unimproved) as at 1 July of the preceding year. The Direct Comparison Method was applied, whereby comparable arm's-length sales were identified, adjusted for improvements (using the partitioning method to isolate the land-only component), time-adjusted to the relevant valuation date, and converted to a rate per square metre for consistent cross-comparison.

Comparable sales were ranked by proximity, similarity of land area, zoning, and constraint profile. Sales requiring the fewest adjustments were assigned the greatest weight in the reconciliation. The indicative land value was derived by applying the weighted average rate per square metre to the subject property's land area.

The resulting indicative value was reconciled against the Valuer-General's Component Factor model. The internal appraisal result is consistent with — or exceeds — the VG assessed value, confirming that the mass appraisal model has not materially overstated the land value for this property.
