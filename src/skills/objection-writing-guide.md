# NSW Valuation Portal — Objection Writing Guide

A field-by-field guide for every text box in the "Reason for objection" step of the NSW Valuation and Objection Portal. Replace all `[bracketed text]` with your actual details.

---

## Golden rules for every text box

- Be factual and specific — use addresses, dates, lot numbers, and dollar figures.
- Explain how each piece of evidence relates to your property.
- Note detrimental features (steep slope, easements, poor access, noise, flooding).
- Upload all supporting documents at the same time — you cannot add more evidence later.
- Do not mention perceived unfairness, rate increases, or personal financial hardship — these are not valid grounds.
- Do not include media articles or general market commentary.
- Always cite the statutory basis: land valuations in NSW are made under the **Valuation of Land Act 1916 (NSW)**. Reference this Act when contesting a valuation.
- Always state the **1 July base date** for the valuation year (e.g. "as at 1 July 2024").
- Always remind the objector of the **60-day window** to lodge an objection from the date of the notice.
- For grounds 1 and 2 (value too high / too low), always state the **assessed rate per m²** — calculated as assessed land value ÷ lot area — alongside the total assessed value.

## 2. My land value is too low

### Opening statement

> I contend that the assessed land value of **[$X]** for **[property address]** (Lot [X], DP [XXXXXX]) is too low. I believe the correct land value as at 1 July [valuing year] should be **[$X]**.

### Supporting rationale

> The following superior attributes of the property support a higher assessed value:
>
> - [e.g. Corner allotment with dual street frontage]
> - [e.g. Elevated position with water views]
> - [e.g. Zoned E1 Local Centre permitting mixed use development]
> - [e.g. Recently rezoned from R2 Low Density Residential to R4 High Density Residential]

### Comparable sales (repeat same table format as above — 3 sales required)

For each sale, explain why the comparable is inferior to your property and how that supports a higher value. Example rationale:

> [Address] is inferior to my property because it is smaller and has no water views. Its adjusted land value of [$X] therefore supports a higher contended value of [$X] for my property.

## 3. The area or dimensions of the land are incorrect

### What to write

> The portal records the land area as **[incorrect area, e.g. 650 m²]**. According to the current Certificate of Title and Deposited Plan [DP number], the correct area is **[correct area, e.g. 612 m²]**.
>
> The recorded frontage is shown as [incorrect dimension]. The correct frontage as per the deposited plan is [correct dimension].
>
> Supporting documents attached:
> - Current Certificate of Title
> - Deposited Plan DP [XXXXXX]
> - [Survey report dated DD/MM/YYYY, if available]

> **Tip:** Certificates of Title and deposited plans can be obtained from NSW Land Registry Services at lrsonline.com.au.

## 4. The description of the land is incorrect

### What to write

> The notice of valuation describes the land as **"[incorrect description, e.g. Lot 1 in DP 12345 — residential]"**. This description is incorrect.
>
> The correct description is: **[correct description, e.g. Lot 1 in DP 12345 — zoned E1 Local Centre under Blacktown Local Environmental Plan 2015]**.

**If the zoning is wrong:**

> The current zoning per the applicable Local Environmental Plan is [correct zone], not [incorrect zone shown on the notice]. A current Section 10.7 Planning Certificate from [Council name] is attached confirming the correct zoning.

**If the lot or plan number is wrong:**

> The correct title reference is Lot [X], DP [XXXXXX], as shown on the attached Certificate of Title issued by NSW Land Registry Services.

> **Tip:** A Section 10.7 Planning Certificate can be obtained from your local council. A Certificate of Title can be obtained from NSW Land Registry Services (lrsonline.com.au).

## 5. This land should have been valued separately

### What to write

> The current valuation aggregates the following lots into a single assessment: **Lot [A], DP [XXXXXX]** and **Lot [B], DP [XXXXXX]**. I contend these should each be valued separately.
>
> Each lot holds a separate Certificate of Title. They are not held under the same ownership or lease arrangement. [Or: "They are not physically contiguous — they are separated by [description, e.g. a public road / another property]."]
>
> [If different owners:] Lot [A] is owned by [Name A] and Lot [B] is owned by [Name B], as evidenced by the attached Certificates of Title.
>
> A separate individual land value should therefore be assessed for each lot.
>
> Documents attached:
> - Certificate of Title for Lot [A], DP [XXXXXX]
> - Certificate of Title for Lot [B], DP [XXXXXX]

## 6. This land should have been valued with other land

### What to write

> The current valuation assesses **Lot [X], DP [XXXXXX]** independently. I contend it should be valued together with **Lot [Y], DP [XXXXXX]** as a single integrated parcel.
>
> The reasons these lots should be valued together are:
>
> - Both lots are held in the same ownership under [the same trust / the same company / the same individual name].
> - They are physically contiguous and share the same access driveway.
> - They are used as a single integrated land holding for the purpose of [describe use, e.g. primary production / a single residential curtilage].
> - They cannot practically be sold or used independently.
>
> Combined area: [X m²].
>
> Documents attached:
> - Certificate of Title for each lot
> - [Site plan or aerial photograph showing the contiguous nature of the holding]
> - [Trust deed or company extract confirming common ownership, if applicable]

## 7. The person on my Notice of Valuation does not own, lease or occupy the land

### What to write

> The notice of valuation names **[incorrect person or entity name]** as the owner/lessee. This is incorrect.
>
> The current registered proprietor of **[property address]** (Lot [X], DP [XXXXXX]) is **[correct legal name exactly as it appears on the Certificate of Title]**.
>
> [If recently transferred:] Title was transferred on [date] as per Dealing No. [XXXXXX] registered with NSW Land Registry Services.
>
> Documents attached:
> - Current Certificate of Title showing the correct registered proprietor

> **Tip:** Consider calling the Valuer General on 1800 110 038 first — this type of error can sometimes be corrected without needing a formal objection.

## 8. The valuations are incorrectly apportioned

### What to write

> The current valuation apportions the total land value of **[$X]** as follows:
> - Lot [A]: [$X]
> - Lot [B]: [$X]
>
> I contend this apportionment is incorrect.
>
> The correct apportionment should reflect [the lot entitlements as set out in the strata plan / the relative land areas of each lot / the relative street frontages].
>
> - Lot [A] has a lot entitlement of [X] / land area of [X m²]
> - Lot [B] has a lot entitlement of [Y] / land area of [Y m²]
>
> Applying the correct ratio of [X:Y] to the total assessed value of [$X], the apportionment should be:
> - Lot [A]: [$X]
> - Lot [B]: [$X]
>
> Documents attached:
> - [Strata plan showing lot entitlements]
> - [Deposited plan showing individual lot areas, if applicable]

## 9. Concessions or allowances are incorrect or missing

### Opening statement

> I contend that the concession/allowance of **[concession name — must match one of the portal radio button options exactly, see list below]** has not been applied to the valuation of **[property address]** (Lot [X], DP [XXXXXX]) and should be.

### Concession types and what to write for each

**Section 62K — Land Tax allowance**

> The land is held in a [fixed trust / discretionary trust] established for [primary production / a qualifying purpose]. It qualifies for a land tax allowance under Section 62K of the Land Tax Management Act 1956. The trust deed is attached confirming the nature and purpose of the trust.

**MDAF 14X — Mixed development apportionment factor**

> The property contains a mixed-use development comprising [X] residential lots and [Y] commercial lots as approved under Development Approval [DA number]. The mixed development apportionment factor (MDAF 14X) should be applied to apportion the site value between the residential and non-residential components. The development approval and strata plan are attached.

**MUAF 14BBA — Mixed use apportionment factor**

> The property is a mixed use development containing both residential and non-residential floor space. The mixed use apportionment factor (MUAF 14BBA) should be applied. The current development approval confirming the floor space ratio and use breakdown is attached.

**Section 14L(1)(A) — On-site allowance**

> The land is burdened by [describe the physical infrastructure, e.g. "a high-voltage electricity transmission line easement running 8 m wide across the northern boundary of the lot"]. This infrastructure is located on the land and physically restricts its development and use, depressing its market value. An on-site allowance under Section 14L(1)(A) should apply. The easement certificate and deposited plan showing the easement location are attached.

**Section 14L(1)(B) — Off-site allowance**

> The land is affected by [describe the off-site infrastructure, e.g. "a high-pressure gas pipeline easement that runs beneath the adjacent road reserve, imposing a development exclusion zone that affects the useable portion of this lot"]. This off-site constraint reduces the development potential and market value of the land. An off-site allowance under Section 14L(1)(B) should apply. Supporting documentation is attached.

**Section 14L(2) — Stratum allowance**

> The land comprises a stratum lot (above or below ground) with restricted use due to [describe, e.g. "the presence of a railway tunnel beneath the site imposing height and load restrictions"]. A stratum allowance under Section 14L(2) should apply. The relevant easement or restriction on use documents are attached.

**Section 14T — Subdividers allowance**

> This land is being progressively subdivided and developed. A subdividers allowance under Section 14T should apply as the land is in the course of development and sale. Documents attached: [development approval, plan of subdivision, evidence of progressive sales].

**Section 585 — Attributable part**

> The valuation should reflect only the attributable part of the land value in accordance with Section 585. [Explain the basis — e.g. "The land is jointly owned and only a proportional share of the land value is attributable to this owner/lessee."] Supporting title documents are attached.

**Section 124 — Heritage Act heritage value**

> The land is listed on the State Heritage Register under the Heritage Act 1977, listing number [SHR number]. Heritage listing restricts the permissible uses and development potential of the land, reducing its market value. A heritage value allowance under Section 124 should apply. The Heritage Council listing and any applicable heritage management plan are attached.

> **Tip:** Select the concession name in the portal's radio buttons first, then use the text box to explain the legal basis and attach the supporting document. The concession will not be applied unless the text box justification is completed.

## Supporting documents checklist

| Reason selected | Key documents to attach |
|---|---|
| Value too high / too low | Benchmark Component Report, comparable sales evidence |
| Area or dimensions incorrect | Certificate of Title, Deposited Plan |
| Description incorrect | Certificate of Title, Section 10.7 Planning Certificate |
| Should be valued separately | Certificate of Title for each lot |
| Should be valued with other land | Both Certificates of Title, site plan, ownership evidence |
| Wrong person on notice | Current Certificate of Title |
| Valuations incorrectly apportioned | Strata plan or Deposited Plan showing lot entitlements/areas |
| Concession missing or incorrect | Trust deed / DA / easement certificate / Heritage listing (depends on type) |
