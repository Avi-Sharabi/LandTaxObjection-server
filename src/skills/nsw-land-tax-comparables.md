# NSW Land Valuation: How the Valuer General Selects Comparable Sales

---

## PART 1: LEGISLATIVE & POLICY FRAMEWORK

### Valuation of Land Act 1916 (NSW)
- Section 6A defines **land value** as the amount the land would realise if sold on the open market, unencumbered by improvements, mortgages or other charges
- The definition assumes a **willing but not anxious buyer and seller**
- The valuation date is fixed at **1 July each year**
- All comparable sales must be interpreted through this statutory lens

### Valuer General's Guidelines & Directions
- The Valuer General issues internal directions to contract valuers on methodology
- These are not always publicly available but underpin every mass valuation cycle
- The **Land and Environment Court** and **NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NCAT)** have developed a significant body of case law on what constitutes a valid comparable

---

## PART 2: MARKET IDENTIFICATION

### Defining the Relevant Market
- Before selecting comps, the valuer must define the **relevant market segment** — who would buy this land and why
- Residential investors, owner-occupiers, developers, and commercial operators are different markets with different pricing logic
- Comps must come from the **same market segment**, not just the same geography

### Delineating the Catchment Area
- Primary zone: same suburb or precinct
- Secondary zone: adjoining suburbs with similar characteristics
- Tertiary zone: broader region, used only when primary/secondary markets are thin
- The catchment expands or contracts depending on **market depth**

### Market Thinness
- In some localities (rural, industrial fringe, specialised) there may be very few sales
- In thin markets, valuers must go back further in time or wider in geography, with appropriate time and location adjustments
- The fewer the sales, the more scrutiny each one receives

---

## PART 3: SALE IDENTIFICATION & DATABASE

### NSW Valuer General Sales Database
- All property sales in NSW are captured via **Contract of Sale** lodgement with NSW Revenue and Land Registry Services
- The database records price, date, parties, legal description, area, and zoning
- Valuers query this database as their primary source

### Initial Screening Criteria
- Same or similar zoning
- Comparable land area (typically within a range, e.g. ±30–50% of subject size)
- Sale date within the relevant time window
- Located within the catchment area

### Flagging Potentially Unusual Sales
- The database flags certain transactions automatically (e.g. related parties, nominal consideration, mortgagee sales)
- Valuers must still independently verify flagged and unflagged sales

---

## PART 4: SALES VERIFICATION (CRITICAL STEP)

### Purpose
- To confirm the sale is a **true open market transaction**
- To identify any conditions, incentives, or circumstances that distort the price

### How Verification is Done
- Contact with selling agent, purchaser, or vendor
- Review of contract conditions and settlement date
- Review of any development approvals or planning changes around the sale date
- Cross-referencing with DA records, planning portals, and title searches

### Grounds for Exclusion After Verification
- Related party or family transfer
- Mortgagee in possession or distressed sale
- Sale subject to unusual lease, option, or encumbrance
- Vendor finance at non-market rates
- Sale packaged with other land or assets
- Compulsory acquisition or pre-acquisition negotiation
- Sale influenced by a specific purchaser's special value (marriage value)
- Off-market or pre-market sale at non-market price
- Incomplete or delayed settlement anomalies
- Sales where price reflects goodwill, business value, or chattels not separated

---

## PART 5: ANALYSIS OF IMPROVED SALES

Most urban sales involve land with buildings. The valuer must extract the **bare land value**.

### Methods to Strip Improvements

**Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC)**
- Estimate the current replacement cost of the building
- Apply depreciation for age, condition, obsolescence
- Subtract from sale price to get residual land value

**Summation Method**
- Land value = Sale price minus value of improvements
- Improvements valued independently using cost or income approaches

**Direct Comparison with Vacant Sales**
- If sufficient vacant land sales exist, use those directly and use improved sales only as a cross-check

### Risks in Improved Sale Analysis
- If the improvement value is overestimated, land value is understated (and vice versa)
- Demolition value or negative improvement value must be considered for derelict structures
- Where a purchaser bought for redevelopment and the building has nil or negative value, this must be reflected

---

## PART 6: ADJUSTMENTS TO COMPARABLE SALES

### Time Adjustment
- Market movement between sale date and 1 July valuation date
- Derived from analysing trends across multiple sales over time
- Applied as a percentage uplift or reduction
- In fast-moving markets this can be significant (e.g. 10–20%+ in a single year)

### Size/Area Adjustment
- Larger lots generally sell at a lower $/m² than smaller lots (the **economies of scale effect**)
- A formal size adjustment curve may be applied, derived from regression analysis of sales in the area

### Location Adjustment
- Micro-location differences: proximity to amenity, busy roads, schools, parks, views
- Even within the same street, values can vary materially

### Shape and Frontage Adjustment
- Regular rectangular lots are more usable and valuable than irregular or narrow lots
- Rear lots (battle-axe) are adjusted downward relative to standard frontage lots
- Corner lots may be adjusted up or down depending on the market

### Topography Adjustment
- Flat land commands a premium over steeply sloping land in most markets
- Sloping land has higher development costs which suppress value

### Services Adjustment
- Fully serviced vs partially serviced vs unserviced
- In fringe areas, the cost of connecting sewer, water, and power is material

### Planning/Zoning Adjustment
- If a comparable has a slightly different FSR, height limit, or permitted uses, adjustments are required
- Rezonings between the sale date and valuation date must be carefully handled

### Constraint Adjustments
- Flood affectation (partial or full)
- Bushfire prone land
- Acid sulfate soils
- Heritage listing or conservation overlay
- Easements and restrictions on title
- Each constraint is assigned a quantified adjustment, often expressed as a % reduction

### Improved vs Vacant Cross-Check
- An adjusted improved sale should be cross-checked against any available vacant land sales to confirm reasonableness

---

## PART 7: RATE ANALYSIS & RECONCILIATION

### Reduction to a Common Unit
- All sales are expressed as **$/m²** (or $/ha for rural land, or $/lot for standard residential subdivisions)
- This normalises for size differences

### Array of Rates
- The valuer assembles an array showing all analysed sales and their adjusted rates
- The subject property's value must sit within or be explainable against that array
- Outliers at the top or bottom of the array are scrutinised

### Reconciliation Process
- No single formula — the valuer exercises **professional judgement** to reconcile the evidence
- Greater weight is given to: more recent sales, closer proximity, less adjustment required, verified arm's length transactions
- Lesser weight to: older sales, more distant sales, sales requiring heavy adjustment

### The Final Concluded Rate
- A single $/m² (or other unit) is concluded for the subject
- Applied to the subject's area to derive the land value
- Cross-checked for reasonableness against the broader tone of the list

## PART 8: MASS APPRAISAL SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS

### Computer Assisted Mass Appraisal (CAMA)
- NSW uses CAMA to value hundreds of thousands of properties simultaneously
- Sales feed into statistical models (often multiple regression analysis or hedonic pricing models)
- The model identifies which attributes (size, location, zoning, etc.) drive value and by how much
- Individual valuers check model outputs against professional judgement

### Stratification
- The mass appraisal process stratifies properties into groups with similar characteristics
- Comparable sales must come from the **same stratum** to be valid model inputs

### Ratio Studies
- After valuations are produced, the Valuer General conducts **ratio studies** — comparing assessed values to subsequent sale prices
- This tests whether the valuation is accurate, uniform, and unbiased across the whole portfolio
- Sales used for ratio studies are separate from those used to calibrate the model

### Uniformity vs Accuracy
- Mass appraisal prioritises **uniformity** (all similar properties treated consistently) as well as accuracy
- A valuation that is individually accurate but inconsistent with neighbours may still be revised

## PART 9: SPECIAL PROPERTY TYPES

### Rural Land
- Comparable sales expressed in $/ha
- Productivity, water entitlements, improvements (fencing, dams, irrigation) stripped out
- Very thin markets — may use sales from a wide geographic area with heavy adjustments

### Commercial & Industrial Land
- Expressed in $/m² of site area
- FSR and height limits are critical — comps must have same or similar development yield
- Passing rental income on improved sales must be carefully stripped

### Englobo / Development Land
- Large parcels valued either as englobo or on a lotted basis
- Lotted basis: project out the subdivision, apply a $/lot rate, deduct development costs and profit/risk
- Must use sales of comparable englobo parcels or analyse lotted sales and work backwards

### Strata Lots
- Unit entitlement as a fraction of the whole site value
- Common property included proportionally
- Sales of strata lots in same or similar schemes used as primary comps

### Stratum Lots
- Air rights or subterranean lots — very specialised, limited comparable evidence
- May require interstate or overseas comparable evidence in rare cases

### Paired Sales Analysis
- Where possible, valuers use **paired sales** — two sales of very similar properties where only one attribute differs
- This helps isolate the value contribution of a specific feature

### Lease Encumbrances
- If land is subject to a long-term lease, the valuer must determine whether the sale reflects the **fee simple unencumbered** interest or a leasehold-affected price

### GST Treatment
- New land sales (e.g. developer selling a new lot) are subject to GST
- Valuers must **strip out GST** from the sale price when using these as comparables

---

## PART 10: THE TONE OF THE LIST & BENCHMARKS

### The "Tone of the List"
- Rather than valuing each property in isolation, the Valuer General establishes a **consistent tone across all properties** in a locality
- Comparable sales anchor that tone — the individual property's value must sit consistently within the pattern of sales evidence across the whole area

### Benchmark Properties
- The Valuer General establishes **benchmark properties** — representative parcels whose value is carefully determined and then used to anchor surrounding valuations
- Comparable sales are used to set these benchmarks first, then the benchmarks ripple outward

## PART 11: OBJECTION & APPEAL CONTEXT

### When You Disagree with the Valuer General's Comps
- Obtain the sales evidence used by accessing the valuation via Service NSW or lodging a formal request under the Act
- Compare their sales against all verified sales in the area for the relevant period
- Identify sales they excluded that should have been included, or included that should have been excluded

### Grounds to Challenge a Comparable
- Not arm's length
- Incorrect time adjustment applied
- Incorrect improvement stripping
- Sale from a different market segment
- Planning conditions materially different
- Physical characteristics not properly adjusted for

### Your Own Comparable Evidence
- In an objection, you can submit your own comparable sales analysis
- Must follow the same methodology — verified, adjusted, reconciled
- The Valuer General must genuinely consider your evidence
- If they reject it, they must give reasons

### NCAT & Land and Environment Court
- At appeal, expert valuers present competing comparable analyses
- The tribunal weighs the evidence and may accept, reject, or blend the competing analyses
- Case law has established principles on what weight to give different types of sales evidence

## SUMMARY: Hierarchy of Evidence

| Priority | Type of Sale | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | Vacant land sales, same suburb, recent | Best — no stripping needed |
| 2 | Improved sales, verified, well-analysed | Acceptable with good improvement data |
| 3 | Vacant land, nearby suburb, recent | Good if market is comparable |
| 4 | Improved sales requiring significant adjustment | Used cautiously |
| 5 | Older sales with time adjustment | Last resort in thin markets |
| 6 | Sales from wider region | Heavy scrutiny, significant adjustments |
